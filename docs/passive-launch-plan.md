# Radiant Life Balance: site review and passive launch plan

Reviewed October 10, 2026. The goal is to help visitors take a useful first step and earn optional affiliate revenue without requiring daily posting or ongoing live events. This is a strategy and implementation backlog, not a promise of viral growth.

## What was reviewed

The live desktop homepage, sample results, generated image-card preview, and the source for quiz navigation, all six areas, report generation, product catalog, newsletter integration, challenge, analytics, and metadata. The paid report's current live generation has not been confirmed successful after the latest timeout update. No newsletter signup, outbound social message, affiliate purchase, or paid transaction was performed in this review. Mobile-device behavior, private Kit automations, affiliate sales, and traffic totals were not independently tested or inspected.

Search-engine retrieval showed an older version of the homepage with the removed ND suffix. Direct browser inspection showed the current Dr. David Lemmon credit. Findings below use the current browser and repository, not that stale search copy.

## The strongest existing assets

- The spacious homepage, rainbow flower, and six circular area graphics provide a recognizable visual identity. Preserve them.
- The quiz is free, short, and can be completed without an account or email address. It now supports Back and Next in the main assessment and all six deep dives.
- Results, a free PDF, a paid report, a challenge, articles, and product links already give visitors several ways to benefit.
- The on-page share summary celebrates a strongest area first and includes the appropriate circular graphic.
- Campaign references and basic event tracking already exist. This is a foundation for measurement, not proof that traffic is growing.

## The immediate recommendation implemented in this review

A single book recommendation appears after the main score and before the report offer. It is selected from the existing affiliate catalog for the number-one priority area. The selection considers the lowest individual statement, not just a category label or an overall score. The card explains the fit, quotes the relevant answer when available, acknowledges ties, and includes one direct merchant link with a nearby affiliate disclosure. It also links to free articles and mentions borrowing from a library.

The main-quiz recommendation uses transparent matching rules, not a fresh paid AI call. This avoids extra API cost on every free quiz completion. The deeper paid report continues to consider both priority deep dives and intake through its existing AI analysis. That analysis can reasonably choose a different product when it has more context.

All 30 statements were checked for an eligible educational product, matching priority area, exact lowest-question evidence, ties, missing original answers, invalid results, and changed answers. This does not establish that every current catalog item is the optimal choice for every person. Expanding weak catalog categories and adding user preference controls are useful later improvements. The current recommendation does not assume a supplement need or recommend a specific investment.

Use merchant current-price links rather than unverified fixed prices or ratings. Product photographs or book covers would improve recognition after properly sourced assets are available. Do not substitute an area logo for an actual book cover.

## The repeatable sharing path

A visitor takes the free quiz, discovers a strength and a starting priority, sees one relevant resource, and optionally shares a comfortable summary with one person. The invited person follows a free quiz link and gets the same useful experience. Either person can opt into a short challenge email series and return later.

The invitation should be about curiosity and connection: ask someone to compare their strongest area or discuss one small next step. Access to free results must not depend on sharing or purchasing. A friend comparison can initially work through conversation and private messages; a server-stored comparison feature is optional future work, not necessary for launch.

An eventual anonymous invitation code can measure referred quiz completions without putting personal scores or questionnaire answers in URLs. Campaign codes already exist but public sharing currently uses the plain quiz link, so this referral measurement is not fully implemented.

## The most passive practical launch

### Stage 1: finish the experience, days 1–7

- Confirm live paid-report generation and recovery from the same paid URL.
- Publish the immediate resource recommendation and verify it on desktop and mobile.
- Make the share-card design match the strength-first summary and provide a dependable sharing fallback.
- Verify the newsletter-to-email flow with an opted-in test address.
- Give visitors a simple free seven-day starting plan before promoting the longer optional challenge. The existing 30-day challenge can remain available.
- Fix canonical URLs and indexing basics before investing heavily in search-driven distribution.

The objective is a dependable useful experience, not a large first-week audience.

### Stage 2: build once, days 8–14

Prepare these durable assets:

1. One clear landing-page invitation: find your strongest area and one place to begin; free, about five minutes, no email required for results.
2. One automatic email series described below.
3. One partner kit: a ready-to-use paragraph, thumbnail, quiz link, optional campaign reference, and a brief guide to discussing results without comparing people competitively.
4. Twelve useful Pinterest images: two distinct ideas per area, each connected to an actual helpful landing page. Ten editable 1000 × 1500 SVG designs already exist in the repository; they are a starting point, not proof of published Pins. Replace generic artwork with the real branding and export suitable PNG or JPEG files before posting.
5. Three launch posts, one short walkthrough video, and a small group of genuine, permissioned testimonials after successful use.
6. Three to six strong evergreen pages or improvements to existing pages addressing questions people actually ask. Avoid making many similar thin pages.

A lightweight partner widget can be a simple linked image or button. Start with this rather than building an elaborate embedded quiz.

### Stage 3: seed it once, days 15–30

Invite a small initial group of people and five to ten relevant partners to try the experience. The numbers are workload limits, not forecasts. Suitable partners include newsletter writers, coaches, book-club organizers, and wellness or community educators whose audiences fit the site. Provide a private campaign reference for each.

Ask partners to put the quiz in something lasting: a welcome email, resource page, onboarding checklist, recurring workshop handout, or newsletter footer. These placements can bring visitors long after a one-time social post. A clear no-email-required free quiz is an easier initial ask than a product promotion.

Place the quiz on your existing relevant website, podcast descriptions, social profile links, and email signature once. Keep your patient communications separate from blanket marketing unless people have appropriately opted in. User-sent invitations to friends are voluntary; the site should not harvest or message their contacts.

Schedule the prepared Pinterest content and a modest set of distinct launch posts. This plan does not require daily original videos, a daily live event, or a large paid advertising campaign. Posting and outreach remain actions for a separately authorized implementation step; nothing was sent or scheduled in this review.

### Stage 4: run and maintain, days 31–90

Let evergreen partner placements, search pages, visitor invitations, and the email sequence work together. Review results once weekly for 20–30 minutes, with one monthly review of links, catalog fit, email delivery, and the purchase/report flow. These are planning estimates, not guaranteed maintenance limits: outages or user support can require more attention.

Improve one bottleneck at a time. If visitors do not start, improve the invitation. If they start but do not finish, examine quiz friction. If they finish but do not invite, simplify or improve the share content. If they click a book but few purchase, check fit, merchant destination, price, and availability. If report failures persist, prioritize reliability before expansion.

## An automatic email sequence with a clear purpose

Use one shared onboarding sequence, with optional area-specific sections, rather than allowing two selected interest tags to launch two competing series. Confirm the Kit plan supports the required automation before designing a large branching system.

| Day 2 | Use an existing strength to support the selected priority. | Choose one repeatable action. |
| Day 4 | Help the person use the recommended resource, with a free alternative. | Read, borrow, or optionally view the resource. |

The current subscription API submits an email and chosen area tags. It does not automatically transmit quiz scores or recreate results on another device. Do not promise an email will contain their exact results until that capability exists. A return-to-results link currently depends on saved browser data. Any future emailed paid-report access link is a private access link, not a public sharing link.

## Revenue and measurement

The service-first revenue path is: useful free result → one relevant optional product → merchant purchase → actual affiliate commission. The paid PDF is an additional option for people who want deeper analysis, not a prerequisite for a resource recommendation.

Do not optimize only for outbound clicks. Use the merchant affiliate dashboard for actual orders and commissions, and reconcile those with the available campaign identifiers. Site click counts cannot establish purchases. Review actual commission rates and AI/payment costs before projecting income or buying ads. No account sales or commission data was available in this review.

A share-button click is an intent signal, not proof that a message was sent or that another person completed a quiz. A useful illustrative measure is new referred completions divided by original completions, after deduplicating where practical. More detailed viral modeling can consider the share rate, recipients reached, click rate, and completion rate. For example, 100 original completions with 20 sharers, three recipients each, a 40% click rate, and a 70% completion rate produce about 17 new completions. These are hypothetical assumptions, not benchmarks or forecasts. This would amplify outside traffic, not sustain growth by itself.

Suggested dashboard measures: main-quiz starts and completion rate; recommendation impressions and outbound clicks by resource; voluntary invite actions; referred visits and completions; opt-ins and email clicks; verified paid purchases; report generation successes and failures; support incidents. Keep raw quiz answers and report intake out of marketing event payloads.

## Design and editorial direction

Preserve generous whitespace, small color accents, circular area graphics, and one primary action per section. Add photography where it contributes to trust, especially a real creator portrait and contextual area-page images. Avoid six-photo collages in the opening or using invented testimonials.

The strength-first image should include the actual flower, an appropriate circular area graphic, a readable invitation, and a visible site address. Square remains useful; provide an optional portrait version for Pinterest and a vertical story layout if demand justifies it. Pinterest's own guidance recommends 2:3, 1000 × 1500 images: https://create.pinterest.com/product-features/how-to-create-pins/ .

Improve existing articles with accurate authorship, sources, useful examples, and internal links rather than using AI to produce a flood of pages. Google warns against many AI-generated pages that add no user value: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content .

## Implementation order

1. Immediate one-resource recommendation — implemented in this review.
2. Confirm paid report generation and recovery; confirm Kit delivery.
3. Strength-first share image, strength-only default, primary invite action.
4. Results-page simplification and clear free/paid distinction.
5. Canonical URLs, sitemap, intentional indexing, and useful page improvements.
6. One automated email sequence with deduplicated entry and a clear subscriber promise.
7. Partner kit, durable link placements, and one batch of evergreen creative assets.
8. Referral and revenue measurement; expand only what brings useful completions and actual revenue.

The most durable launch is a small connected system: helpful free assessment, relevant optional resource, easy voluntary invitation, automatic follow-up, and permanent referral placements. It still needs initial seeding, honest measurement, and occasional maintenance.
